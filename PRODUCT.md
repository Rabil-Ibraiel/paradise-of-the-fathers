# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

web

## Users

General readers, members of Church of the East communities, and readers seeking
an accessible entry into Syriac Christian history and spirituality.

## Product Purpose

The Paradise of the Fathers is a new, simple, illustrated collection that helps
readers encounter principal saints associated with the Church of the East. It
respectfully borrows the famous title of the classic Syriac book without
presenting itself as an edition or translation of that historical work. Each
profile must work both as a devotional introduction and as a historically
responsible starting point for further study.

## Positioning

The archive joins spiritual reflection to clearly labeled historical context,
distinguishing received tradition and hagiography from modern historical claims.

## Operating Context

Readers browse a visual archive, choose one saint, read a concise spiritual
introduction, continue into historical context, and follow external scholarly
sources when they want to study further. Saints, manuscripts, books, and the
story of the project's name live on separate, directly linkable pages; the
homepage shows featured selections only.

## Capabilities and Constraints

- Every saint in the archive has a dedicated, directly linkable page.
- The site is statically generated and deployed through GitHub Pages.
- Historical and hagiographical claims must be distinguished in the copy.
- The project is independent and is not an official publication of a Church
  jurisdiction.
- Website builds and deployments run on GitHub-hosted infrastructure.

## Brand Commitments

Preserve the name “The Paradise of the Fathers,” the English and Syriac identity,
the manuscript-inspired visual language, the Assyria Alqosh East Syriac typeface,
and a reverent, clear voice. Use documented historical or devotional images
with visible provenance; where none is responsibly available, use a symbolic
monogram rather than a synthetic portrait.

## Evidence on Hand

The repository contains a six-saint archive, a CC0 devotional icon of Saints
Addai and Mari, and a public-domain medieval icon of Isaac the Syrian. Image
provenance is recorded in `public/images/ATTRIBUTIONS.md`. Scholarly records from
Syriaca.org, the Gorgias Encyclopedic Dictionary of the Syriac Heritage, and
holding-library catalogues provide starting references. Syri.ac, hosted by the
University of Oklahoma, provides an annotated gateway to open-access editions,
bibliography, manuscript catalogues, and digitised Syriac witnesses. The site
links into those records and writes its own concise descriptions rather than
reproducing the Syri.ac database.

## Product Principles

- Welcome devotion without presenting tradition as documented chronology.
- Make scholarship approachable without flattening complexity.
- Give every saint a memorable, navigable place in the archive.
- Prefer careful source links to unsupported certainty.
- Keep the reading experience calm, legible, and respectful.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Profiles must remain keyboard navigable, responsive, readable with reduced
motion, and explicit about the educational status of the content.
